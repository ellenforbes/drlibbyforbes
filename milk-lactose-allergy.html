Cow's milk allergy



Tree nut allergies require careful risk management because exposure to even tiny amounts of tree nuts can trigger allergic reactions, including life‑threatening anaphylaxis, in sensitised individuals. According to the Australasian Society of Clinical Immunology and Allergy (ASCIA), the cornerstone of managing a confirmed tree nut allergy is strict avoidance of the specific nuts that cause reactions and education on recognising and responding to allergic symptoms. People with this allergy should work with their healthcare provider to develop and carry an ASCIA Action Plan for Anaphylaxis, which outlines personalised instructions for early recognition and emergency treatment, including when to use an adrenaline (epinephrine) auto‑injector. In addition to having an action plan, risk minimisation involves strategies such as reading ingredient labels diligently, communicating the allergy to food providers and caregivers, reducing risk of cross‑contact in food preparation, and ensuring environments such as schools or childcare services have appropriate allergy and emergency plans in place. These combined avoidance and preparedness measures help reduce the chance of accidental exposure and ensure prompt, effective responses if a reaction occurs.
